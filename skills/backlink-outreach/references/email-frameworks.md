# Email frameworks

Two rules on top of everything below:

- **The first line must prove the article was read.** If the sentence could
  have been written without opening the page, it is not a first line.
- **Never position yourself as an alternative to a partner you depend on.**
  Presenting yourself as a supplier's replacement is false, and it is the
  fastest way to lose a partner who could have linked.

Adapt every framework to the actual product and the exact page. Keep the first
message concise.

## Inbound request: interested, request materials

**Subject:** Re: [original subject]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for reaching out. I reviewed [Product] and [specific page], and the fit
looks natural: [Product] helps users [their step], while [Your Product]
supports [the adjacent step].

To evaluate the strongest placement, could you send a short approved
description, the two or three features you want emphasised, your preferred
landing page, any current plan or offer details, and two or three current
screenshots or a short demo? Suggested captions and accuracy notes help too.
Visuals let us explain the workflow more accurately than a text-only mention.

Here is our reference pack with approved descriptions, links, screenshots and
accuracy notes: [reference pack]. Please adapt the wording to fit your article.

When your placement is live, send the URL and we will review the section and
use your materials to prepare or update the most natural coverage on our side.

Best,
[Name]

## Outbound request: a specific content gap

**Subject:** [Specific workflow addition] for "[Article title]"

Hi [Name],

I read "[Article title]" ([URL]). [One specific, truthful observation about
what the article helps readers do.]

The next step is [the missing workflow step]. That is where [Your Product]
fits: [one or two sentences describing the complementary workflow, no inflated
claims].

A natural placement could be [specific section]. If useful, here is our
reference pack with approved wording and screenshots: [reference pack].

We also publish about [relevant topic], where [their product] could be a useful
[before/after] resource. If you send an approved description, priority
features, preferred landing page and a few current screenshots, we can review
the best article or start preparing a relevant piece and share the proposed
placement.

Best,
[Name]

## Their placement is live

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the update. I reviewed the live article at [URL]. [Confirm accuracy,
or list concise corrections.]

We have your materials and will use them to prepare or update the most natural
[topic] coverage on our side. If there is a particular feature or page you want
emphasised, send it over.

Thanks again,
[Name]

## Our placement is live

Hi [Name],

We published the new guide and included [their product] here:

[URL]

It appears in [section] as the [workflow role]. Please review the wording,
screenshots and links, and tell me if any factual detail needs correcting. We
keep final editorial control but are glad to fix inaccuracies.

Thanks,
[Name]

## Follow-up

Send only when delivery succeeded and the fit remains strong. One, then stop.

Hi [Name],

Following up on the note below about adding [Your Product] to [specific
article]. The suggested workflow is still [their step] then [your step].

If this is relevant to your content plans, I can send a short reference pack
and screenshots. If not, no problem.

Best,
[Name]

## Phrases to avoid

- "You link to us and we will link to you."
- "Guaranteed SEO boost."
- "We love your product", when it was not tested.
- Unverified traffic or ranking claims.
- "We already wrote the article", unless it is genuinely drafted or published.
- "We will definitely include you", before editorial review.
