# Reciprocal placement: how you go first

The strongest version of this whole pipeline. You publish their mention
**before** asking for anything, then send the live URL with nothing attached to
it.

Two reasons it beats asking first. An email with no request in it gets read
differently. And it is the only form of reciprocity that never requires
promising a link before editorial review, which you are not allowed to do.

## The gate, before anything else

**Editorial independence outranks the partnership.** You link to a partner when
the link helps the reader of that specific article. If the reader benefit
cannot be stated in one sentence, there is no placement, and the outreach ends
at the email.

Never manufacture a paragraph to hold a link. A section that exists only to
carry a partner reads as a link scheme to a search engine, and worse, it reads
that way to the partner.

## Step A: pick the host article

In this order:

1. **An existing guide that already covers the workflow they sit in.** Thicken
   it in place. **Never change a live URL.**
2. **A backlog target from the content library** you were going to write
   anyway.
3. **A net-new guide**, only when their category is a genuine gap in your
   coverage and the topic answers something you already care about ranking for.

Reject any post whose only reason to exist is the link.

## Step B: decide the placement shape

- **Contextual mention** inside a section they genuinely belong in. Default.
- **A row in a comparison or tool table**, when the guide already has one.
- **A "before this step" or further-reading pointer.** Weakest. Only when
  nothing better exists.

Anchor text is their product name or a natural noun phrase. Not "click here",
not an exact-match keyword.

## Step C: what you may say about them

Sources, and nothing outside them:

- their approved description and materials, if they sent any;
- their public docs, pricing or homepage, cited as of a stated date;
- **your own measured test**, printed with the date and the exact input.

Never: invented usage numbers, invented praise, "we love", "the best", uptime
or accuracy claims you did not run yourself, or anything about their funding,
customers or traffic.

If their materials have not arrived, say so in the brief and use public facts
only.

## Step D: brief `content-thicken`

Hand it exactly this. Do not let it improvise partner facts.

```
target article    : <existing slug, or "new" plus the topic>
partner           : <name>, one sourced line on who they are
section           : <where the mention goes>
reader benefit    : <one sentence, this is the gate>
anchor text       : <text> -> <destination URL>
approved claims   : <list, each with its source>
banned claims     : <list, from their accuracy notes or yours>
materials         : received | not received (public facts only)
```

It drafts, validates deterministically, and **stops at preview**. Preview is
where a human reads it, not where it publishes.

## Step E: two reviews, in order

1. **Yours.** Does it help the reader. Is every partner fact sourced. Does it
   pass the fact check.
2. **Theirs, when practical.** Send the preview and ask for factual correction
   only. Say plainly: you keep editorial control, and you will fix
   inaccuracies.

**Publishing is its own authorisation.** Drafting is always allowed. Publishing
never is by default.

## Step F: deliver

**Subject:** Added [Partner] to our [topic] guide

```
Hi [Name],

We published our [topic] guide and included [Partner] in it:

[URL]

It sits in [section] as [workflow role], and the wording came from
[their materials / your public docs, dated]. If any detail is wrong, tell me
and I will fix it. We keep editorial control, but accuracy is not negotiable.

There is no ask attached to that.

Separately: if a mention of [us] ever fits one of your pieces, our reference
pack is attached. Approved descriptions, the boundary of what we do and do not
do, and images you are free to use.

Best,
[Name]
```

Attach the file. Do not link a drive folder.

Three things make this email work:

- **"There is no ask attached to that" is said out loud.** Leaving it implied
  wastes the whole move, because the reader is scanning for the catch.
- The ask comes second and is conditional: *if it ever fits*. Never "in
  exchange", never "reciprocal link", never anything that reads as an invoice.
- The reference pack does the selling later, when they actually sit down to
  write. That is what it is for.

## Step G: then, and only then, follow up

If nothing appears in two to three weeks, **one** follow-up. Then stop.

**Do not remove your placement because they did not reciprocate.** Pulling a
link when a favour is not returned is the definition of a link scheme, and it
makes the guide worse for the reader who was the reason it was there. If the
placement was correct on the day it published, it stays correct.

## Tracker states this path walks

```
interested -> materials_requested -> materials_received
           -> our_content_drafting -> our_placement_live
           -> (their_placement_live) -> verified
```

The two placement columns move independently. Yours going live proves nothing
about theirs.
